Before you compress: a short checklist

Most rejected uploads fail for a small, avoidable reason. Run through these points once, and the compressed file will usually pass on the first attempt.

  1. Read the portal's limit exactly. Note the unit (KB or MB), whether it is a maximum or a range, and whether the file must be a PDF. A portal that says "200 KB" means 200 KB or less, so aim a little under it.
  2. Combine pages first, compress second. If the portal takes one file for several certificates, put them in one PDF with the image to PDF tool, then compress the result. Compressing each page separately and then joining them can push the total over the limit again.
  3. Scan for the screen, not for print. A scan at 150 to 200 DPI is easy to read on a screen and far smaller than one at 600 DPI. Grayscale is enough for marksheets that have no coloured seals.
  4. Pick the largest target that is allowed. A higher target keeps more detail. Choose the biggest preset that is still under the portal's limit, and only go lower if the result is still too large.
  5. Check the result before you upload. Open the download and zoom in on roll numbers, dates, signatures and stamps. If any of them is hard to read, a verifier will find it hard too, so try a higher target or rescan the page.
  6. Keep your original. Compression is meant for the upload copy. Store the untouched scan in a safe place for later applications and for the document verification round.

Why a PDF can stay large

A PDF made from photos or scans is mostly picture data, and picture data can be reduced. Very large pages, many pages, or photographs taken at the full resolution of a modern phone camera are the usual causes of large files. If a file is still above the limit at the lowest target you can use, split the document into the sections the portal asks for, or retake the photos with the page filling the frame so that no pixels are wasted on the desk around it.

What happens to your file

The document is read, compressed and rebuilt inside your browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server, so a marksheet or identity proof never leaves your device. Closing the tab clears it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do recruitment portals limit PDF certificate sizes to 200 KB or 300 KB?

Portals like UPSC and state public service commissions receive hundreds of thousands of document uploads simultaneously. Capping PDFs to 200 KB or 300 KB ensures fast server processing and efficient database storage.

Does this tool upload my sensitive certificates to external servers?

No. ApplyReady processes and recompresses PDF documents 100% inside your local web browser. Your marks, personal details, and certificates never touch any external server.

How does the tool ensure text on the compressed PDF remains readable?

The tool uses selective JPEG stream quantization. It calculates the minimum compression required to satisfy your exact KB target without discarding high-frequency text contours, ensuring dates, roll numbers, and grades stay legible.

Can I convert photos of certificates directly to a compressed PDF?

Yes. You can upload photo scans (JPG or PNG) directly. The compressor automatically compiles them into an A4 PDF guaranteed to be under your chosen target file size.

What is the typical PDF size limit for UPSC Civil Services online application?

UPSC typically requires scanned document PDFs (such as photo identity proof and age relaxation certificates) to be between 20 KB and 300 KB.

What should I do if my PDF file is still too large?

If your original document was scanned at ultra-high 600 DPI, choose a lower target like 200 KB or 150 KB. The tool will automatically scale pixel dimensions to comfortably meet the limit.
