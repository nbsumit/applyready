Typical portal limits

Every job portal, university application form and employer onboarding system sets its own limit, and the number is printed next to the upload slot. As a rough guide, limits for a single certificate or ID scan are often a few hundred kilobytes, for example 200 KB, 300 KB or a range such as 50 to 300 KB, while a resume or a combined "supporting documents" file may be allowed 1 or 2 MB. These figures are illustrative: always use the limit on the form in front of you, and note whether it is a maximum or a range with a minimum as well.

A resume exported as text from a word processor or the resume builder is usually far below any limit. The large files are almost always scans and phone photos of paper documents, which is what this tool is for.

Before you compress: a short checklist

Most rejected uploads fail for a small, avoidable reason. Run through these points once, and the compressed file will usually pass on the first attempt.

  1. Read the portal's limit exactly. Note the unit (KB or MB), whether it is a maximum or a range, and whether the file must be a PDF. A portal that says "200 KB" means 200 KB or less, so aim a little under it.
  2. Combine pages first, compress second. If the portal takes one file for several certificates, put them in one PDF with the image to PDF tool, then compress the result. Compressing each page separately and then joining them can push the total over the limit again.
  3. Scan for the screen, not for print. A scan at 150 to 200 DPI is easy to read on a screen and far smaller than one at 600 DPI. Greyscale is enough for mark sheets and letters that have no coloured seals.
  4. Pick the largest target that is allowed. A higher target keeps more detail. Choose the biggest preset that is still under the portal's limit, and only go lower if the result is still too large.
  5. Check the result before you upload. Open the download and zoom in on registration numbers, dates, signatures and stamps. If any of them is hard to read, the person checking your application will find it hard too, so try a higher target or rescan the page.
  6. Keep your original. Compression is meant for the upload copy. Store the untouched scan in a safe place for later applications and for document checks after an offer.

How it works, and what changes

Each page is drawn by your browser at the resolution you choose and saved as a JPEG, at the highest quality that fits that page's share of your limit. The new PDF has the same number of pages, in the same order, with the same page sizes and orientation, and it is opened again and checked before you can download it. Because every page becomes a picture, text in the result can no longer be selected or searched: this is the right trade for scans and phone photos, which are pictures already, and a poor one for a PDF exported from a word processor, which is usually small enough as it is. Files over 100 MB or 300 pages are refused with a message, and the work can be cancelled at any time.

To bring several documents in under one limit, put them together with Merge PDF first, then compress the result. To drop pages the portal does not ask for, use Organise PDF pages, or send each section to its own upload slot with Split PDF.

Why a PDF can stay large

A PDF made from photos or scans is mostly picture data, and picture data can be reduced. Very large pages, many pages, or photographs taken at the full resolution of a modern phone camera are the usual causes of large files. If a file is still above the limit at the lowest target you can use, split the document into the sections the portal asks for, or retake the photos with the page filling the frame so that no pixels are wasted on the desk around it. Getting a scanned PDF under a portal's limit explains the order of changes in more detail.

What happens to your file

The document is read, compressed and rebuilt inside your browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server, so a mark sheet or ID proof never leaves your device. Closing the tab clears it.

Questions

Why do job portals limit PDF sizes?

Smaller files upload faster on slow connections, take less storage, and open quickly for the person reviewing applications. The limit is set by each portal, so check the number printed next to the upload slot.

Will the text in my PDF stay selectable?

No. To reach a size limit, every page is redrawn as an image and saved as a JPEG. The pages look the same, but their text can no longer be selected, copied or searched. This suits scanned documents and photos of documents best; a PDF exported from a word processor is usually small enough already.

How does the tool keep the pages readable?

Each page is drawn at the resolution you choose, 150 DPI by default, and saved at the highest JPEG quality that fits that page's share of your limit. Only when the lowest quality is still too large is the resolution lowered, down to 50 DPI, and the result says so. Always zoom in on the smallest text before you upload.

Does compressing change the number or size of pages?

No. The compressed PDF has the same number of pages, in the same order, with the same page sizes and orientation. The tool opens the new file again and checks this before it offers the download.

Can I compress a photo of a certificate directly?

Yes. Choose a JPG, PNG or WebP photo and it becomes a one-page A4 PDF under your limit. If the limit cannot be met at a readable quality, the tool tells you the smallest size it reached.

What if my PDF is already under the limit?

The tool keeps your original file unchanged and says so. Compressing a file that already fits would only lose detail. It also keeps the original when compressing would make the file larger, which can happen with PDFs that are mostly text.

What should I do if my PDF file is still too large?

Remove pages the form does not ask for, turn on Black and white, choose a lower resolution, or split the document across the upload slots the portal provides.

Does this tool upload my certificates to a server?

No. ApplyReady reads and recompresses the PDF inside your web browser. Your certificates, marks and personal details never leave your device.
