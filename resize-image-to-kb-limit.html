How to use this page

Pick Under 10 KB, Under 100 KB or Under 200 KB in the tool, or choose Custom size and type the exact minimum and maximum from your form. Then set the pixel size the form gives; the limit only decides how hard the compressor has to work. The sections below say which official forms use each limit and what the compressor produced on our synthetic test images (a 1200 × 1600 px portrait and a 1600 × 900 px phone photo of a signature, both in /img/samples/), measured in Chromium 141 on 24 October 2026. Jump to 10 KB, 100 KB or 200 KB. For the two most common limits there are dedicated pages: 20 KB and 50 KB.

10 KB: small signatures, and the minimum many forms set

Ten kilobytes (10,240 bytes) appears in official documents more often as a minimum than as a maximum. SSC and IBPS signatures must be at least 10 KB (SSC CGL 2026 notice, para 9.6; IBPS CRP PO/MT-XVI, Annexure III), and NEET (UG) 2026 sets 10 KB as the lower bound for the photograph and signature (Information Bulletin, page 16). As a maximum, the Protean guidelines for the DSC-based PAN application allow a signature scan of "Max. 10 KB", in colour JPEG at 200 DPI, 2 × 4.5 cm.

If a form gives 10 KB as a minimum and your file is lighter, enter 10 in Minimum (KB) and the tool pads the JPEG to reach it.

100 KB: declarations, triple signatures and larger photos

One hundred kilobytes (102,400 bytes) is the ceiling for uploads that carry more ink or more pixels than a normal signature.

200 KB: high-resolution photos

Two hundred kilobytes (204,800 bytes) is generous for a photo. It lets a portal ask for a large, sharp image while still keeping uploads small.

With 200 KB to spend, even the full 1200 × 1600 photo kept a quality of 0.86. The risk at this limit is the other way round: a small 350 × 350 px photo was 21.6 KB, so check the form's minimum as well as its maximum.

If the portal still rejects the file

  • Below the minimum: enter the form's minimum in Minimum (KB); do not enlarge the image to gain bytes.
  • Wrong dimensions: the result table reads the pixel size from the finished file. If the form gives centimetres, read pixels, centimetres and DPI.
  • Over the limit by a few bytes: with 1 KB counted as 1,024 bytes, 100 KB is 102,400 bytes. Set the maximum 1 KB lower if unsure how a portal counts.
  • Anything else: match the portal's message in upload errors and their fixes.

Frequently asked questions

Which forms use a 10 KB, 100 KB or 200 KB limit?

In the official documents we checked: the DSC-based PAN application allows a signature of up to 10 KB; the IBPS handwritten declaration is 50 to 100 KB; NEET (UG) 2026 allows a 10 to 100 KB signature and a 10 to 200 KB photograph. Each table on this page links the source.

Should I lower the quality or the pixel size?

The pixel size, first. Set the size the form asks for; the tool then keeps the highest quality that fits. In our tests a 1200 × 1600 photo needed quality 0.73 to fit 100 KB, but an 800 × 1000 photo kept 0.89.

What if my file is smaller than the form's minimum?

Enter the minimum in Minimum (KB). The tool pads the JPEG with comment data to reach it without changing the picture.

Is 100 KB the same as 100,000 bytes?

Not necessarily. With 1 KB counted as 1,024 bytes, 100 KB is 102,400 bytes. The result shows the exact byte count.

Is my file uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser on your device.
