UPSC Photo and Signature Size, 2026

The UPSC portal checks four things: the file type, its size in KB, its file name and, for the signature, its pixel range. Here is each rule as the portal instructions state it, and what changed with the live photo.

What the UPSC portal asks for

UPSC's notices do not print the upload limits. The Civil Services 2026 notice sends candidates to the instructions on the application portal, upsconline.nic.in, and the figures below come from the two instruction PDFs published there. They apply to the Common Application Form (CAF), which the CSE 2026 notice describes as "common to all examination applications".

The photo has no official pixel size. The portal checks the file's size in KB, its type and its name. That is why this page's photo preset is labelled "suggested": it uses 350 × 450 px, a passport-photo shape, because the tool needs some size to produce a file. Any size that keeps the face clear and the file between 20 and 200 KB meets the stated rule.

The signature does have one. The portal note says "The height and width of signature image must be between 350 and 500 pixels". The preset's 400 × 400 px sits inside that range; 350 × 500 px is also valid if three signatures stacked vertically fit better in a taller image.

One notice still shows an older limit. The Engineering Services 2027 notice (No. 02/2027-ENGG., 16 September 2026, Appendix-IIA) says each file "should not exceed 300 KB" and must be at least 20 KB. The same notice tells candidates to follow the portal instructions, and the portal accepts photos only up to 200 KB and signatures up to 100 KB, so use the portal limits.

Where the photo and signature fit in the UPSC application

  1. Account, Universal Registration and CAF are done once. The CSE 2026 notice describes "four cards/modules, three of which namely, Account creation, Universal Registration and Common Application Form are common to all examination applications." The URN "has to be registered only once in lifetime."
  2. Photo & Signature is the last CAF profile. Other CAF profiles can be filled in any order "except the last one i.e. Photo and Signature profile" (General Instructions, p. 13).
  3. Upload, then capture. Candidates "are required to upload their photograph and also capture their live photograph while filling up the Common Application Form" (CSE 2026 notice, Note 2, p. 2). The live photo is matched with the uploaded one.
  4. File names are checked. Name the files exactly photo.jpg and signature.jpg; the portal refuses other names.
  5. No changes after submission. "No correction /alteration/modification in any field(s) of the Application Form is allowed after submission" (CSE 2026 notice, p. 2). The URN profile can be edited once, but the change does not reach applications already submitted.

If an upload is refused, UPSC's FAQ (Q. 21) says to check the file against the upload instructions: "Any deviation from the instructions will not allow the document to be uploaded." For the CSE 2026 window the notice listed a helpline, 011-24041001, and the email upscsoap@nic.in, for problems including document uploads.

Preparing the two files

Photo. The instructions ask for a frontal view with "head in the centre and both ears should be visible", and say a photograph "with dark background or in uniform, or with eyes hidden under coloured or dark glasses will not be accepted." The portal also rejects black and white photos. Crop so the face fills about three quarters of the frame; the 75% rule is easier to meet in a tight crop than in a wide one. Our synthetic test portrait, resized to 350 × 450 px, was 26.5 KB at quality 0.95: far inside the 20 to 200 KB range, so this preset keeps the highest quality.

Triple signature. Sign three times on one sheet with a gap between each, scan or photograph the sheet, and crop to the three signatures. The instructions require "exactly three signatures", properly oriented, "on plain white paper (no lines or colours)". At 400 × 400 px our test signature photo came out at 29.7 KB at quality 0.95, above the 20 KB minimum without padding. A cleaner image with more white space can fall below 20 KB; the preset pads it to the minimum if so. Cleaning the paper first with the signature cleaner helps the black ink stand out.

Measurements are from our test images, in Chromium on 24 October 2026. For the difference between pixel size and file size, see pixels, cm, DPI and KB; certificates for the CAF can be combined and sized with image to PDF and compress PDF.

Frequently asked questions: UPSC

What is the UPSC photo size in KB?

20 KB to 200 KB, in JPG format, with the file named photo. This is stated in the Instructions for Uploading the Photo & Signature on upsconline.nic.in and shown on the portal itself.

Does UPSC require a particular pixel size for the photo?

No pixel size is stated in the portal instructions or the 2026 notices. The rules are about content: colour, plain white background, face covering at least 75% of the photo, both ears visible. This page's 350 × 450 px preset is a suggestion, not an official size.

What are the UPSC signature requirements?

Sign three times, one below the other, in black ink on plain white paper, and scan all three as one JPG of 20 KB to 100 KB named signature. Its height and width must each be between 350 and 500 pixels.

Does the UPSC photo need my name and date on it?

No. The current instructions do not ask for a name or date, and say the "Photograph is NOT to be signed." A live photo is captured on the portal and matched with the uploaded one instead.

Which limit is right: 200 KB or 300 KB?

Use 200 KB for the photo and 100 KB for the signature. The ESE 2027 notice's appendix still mentions 300 KB, but it also tells candidates to follow the portal's instructions, and the portal accepts only up to 200 KB and 100 KB.

Is the photo ID a PDF?

No. The portal asks for the photo ID as a JPG of 20 KB to 200 KB named id_card. Certificates such as the matriculation certificate are PDFs of 50 KB to 300 KB.
