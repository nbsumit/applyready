Which official forms use a 50 KB limit

Fifty kilobytes is a photo limit, and in the documents we checked it always comes with a minimum of 20 KB. That minimum is what catches people out, because a small photo is naturally much lighter than 20 KB.

UploadOfficial limitSize givenSource
IBPS photograph (PO, Clerk, RRB, SO, 2026–27 cycle)20 to 50 KB200 × 230 px preferred (4.5 × 3.5 cm)CRP PO/MT-XVI notification, 1 July 2026, Annexure III
IBPS left thumb impression20 to 50 KB, JPG/JPEG240 × 240 px at 200 DPI (3 × 3 cm)Same notification, Annexure III

Other exams do not use 50 KB for the photo. SSC no longer takes an uploaded photo at all: its 2025–26 notices capture a live photo during the application (SSC page). UPSC allows 20 to 200 KB and NEET (UG) 10 to 200 KB; see the UPSC and NEET pages.

What fits in 50 KB: measured with this tool

50 KB is 51,200 bytes. We ran this page's compressor, with a 50 KB maximum and JPEG output, on the synthetic test portrait (1200 × 1600 px, 428.9 KB) and test signature (1600 × 900 px) in our sample set. The tool starts at quality 0.95 and lowers it only when the file is too large. Measured in Chromium 141 on 24 October 2026.

Up to about 400 × 530 px, our test portrait fitted in 50 KB at full quality, with room to spare. The full-resolution 1200 × 1600 photo also fitted, but only at quality 0.55, where fine detail such as hair starts to smear. The IBPS size, 200 × 230 px, is the opposite problem: the photo is far lighter than the 20 KB minimum. With 20 entered as the minimum, the tool padded the same JPEG to exactly 20.0 KB without changing the picture. Real phone photos carry more detail than our synthetic portrait, so expect somewhat larger numbers.

Troubleshooting a 50 KB upload

  • "File size should be at least 20 KB". Common with the IBPS 200 × 230 px photo. Enter 20 in Minimum (KB) and process again; do not enlarge the photo to gain bytes.
  • Accepted size, but the face looks smeared. The pixel size is too large for the limit. Use the size the notice gives, or a passport-photo size such as 350 × 450 px, which fitted at full quality in our test.
  • The portal says the dimensions are wrong. Read the result table: the dimensions are measured from the finished file. If the form gives centimetres, convert with the method in pixels, centimetres and DPI.
  • The thumb impression is too light. IBPS asks for black or blue ink on white paper. Photograph it in daylight and crop to the print before resizing to 240 × 240 px.
  • The file was fine on the phone but rejected by the portal. Messaging apps recompress photos. Transfer the original file, not a forwarded copy; see upload errors and their fixes.

Other limits: 20 KB for signatures, and 10, 100 and 200 KB on one page. For taking a photo that compresses well in the first place, read taking a passport-style photo at home.

Frequently asked questions: 50 KB

Which forms use a 50 KB photo limit?

In the official documents we checked, IBPS uses 20 to 50 KB for the photograph (200 × 230 px preferred) and for the left thumb impression (240 × 240 px). UPSC and NEET (UG) allow larger photos, and SSC captures the photo live during the application.

Why does the portal say my photo is too small?

Because it is below the minimum. At 200 × 230 px our test photo was only 9.1 KB at full quality, under the IBPS minimum of 20 KB. Enter the minimum in Minimum (KB) and the tool pads the JPEG to reach it without changing the picture.

What pixel size gives the best 50 KB photo?

The size the notice gives. Without one, a passport-photo size such as 350 × 450 px fitted in 50 KB at full quality in our test, while the full 1200 × 1600 photo needed quality 0.55.

Is 50 KB the same as 50,000 bytes?

Not necessarily. With 1 KB counted as 1,024 bytes, 50 KB is 51,200 bytes. The result shows the exact byte count; if unsure how a portal counts, set the maximum to 49 KB.

Should I use PNG or WebP for a 50 KB photo?

Use JPEG unless the form names another format. Exam portals in India commonly ask for JPG/JPEG, and PNG cannot be compressed by lowering quality.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The resizing and compression run in your browser on your device.
