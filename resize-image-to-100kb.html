Tips for Handwritten Declarations Under 100 KB

  • Write on Plain White Paper: Ruled/lined paper creates dark horizontal lines that waste precious bytes during JPEG encoding. Use plain white unruled paper.
  • Dark Black Pen: Use a dark black ballpoint pen for bold letter contrast.
  • Proper Cropping: Crop out tables, bedspreads, or desks from the photo frame so only the written text area is encoded.

How many pixels can 100 KB afford?

A 100 KB file is 102,400 bytes, or 819,200 bits. That is twice the budget of a 50 KB limit, which is enough for a larger portrait or a handwritten page if you choose the dimensions sensibly.

Rule of thumb for photographs: 2.5 or more bits per pixel is comfortable, 1.5 to 2.5 is workable, below 1.5 looks soft or blocky.

Declarations and handwritten notes. An 800 x 400 declaration has 320,000 pixels and gets about 2.6 bits each. Handwriting is high-contrast on a plain page, so it stays readable at this budget as long as the paper is white and the pen is dark. Photograph it flat and in even light, and crop to the writing.

Portraits. A 600 x 800 portrait gets about 1.7 bits per pixel, which is workable but soft; dropping to 400 x 400 or similar gives a noticeably cleaner face. A 1000 x 1000 image at this limit gets under one bit per pixel, which is why very large dimensions with a 100 KB limit look blocky.

Choose the size the form needs, not the largest it allows. Larger dimensions do not add information a reviewer can use, but they do take bytes away from every pixel.

  • For handwriting, use dark ink and white paper, and avoid ruled lines.
  • Check readability at 100 percent zoom: you should be able to read every word.
  • If the first result is too soft, reduce the pixel size a step instead of raising the limit.

Frequently Asked Questions: 100 KB File Compression

Common queries on 100 KB documents and declarations.

Why is 100 KB recommended for IBPS declarations?

IBPS specifies a file size range between 50 KB and 100 KB for handwritten declarations. Targeting close to 90–95 KB ensures high resolution (approx 800 × 400 pixels) so the verification officer can clearly read every word of your text.

Can I use this for UPSC square photos?

Yes. UPSC allows photos between 20 KB and 200 KB. Setting 100 KB produces a sharp square image well within official boundaries.
