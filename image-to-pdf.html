How to turn photos of documents into one clean PDF

Use this tool when a portal asks for several pages (mark sheets, a certificate and its back side, an ID card) as one PDF, and all you have are photos or scans. Each image becomes one page, in the order shown in the list.

  1. Photograph each page flat, in even light, with the phone parallel to the paper. Crop away the table around the page.
  2. Add all the images at once, then check the numbered list. Drag a page by its handle, or use the arrows, until the order matches what the portal asks for.
  3. Rotate sideways pages with the rotate button so every page opens upright.
  4. Pick the page size and quality. A4 with a small margin suits most uploads; Same as each image keeps each photo's own proportions. Start with Balanced.
  5. Create the PDF and check its size against the portal's limit before you upload.

Real output sizes

We combined the two images in our sample set, a 1200 × 1600 px photo (428.9 KB) and a 1600 × 900 px photo of a signature (251.5 KB), into a two-page A4 PDF with small margins. The quality setting decides the size:

Quality settingPDF size (2 pages)
High, for clear text scans618.4 KB
Balanced, for most forms227.5 KB
Compact, for a smaller file136.3 KB

When a portal's limit is lower still, run the finished PDF through the PDF compressor: the Compact file above came down from 136.3 KB to 77.3 KB with a 100 KB target. Measure your own files; a page full of small print needs more bytes than our test images.

Common mistakes

  • Pages in the wrong order. The PDF follows the list exactly. Check the page numbers before you create it, not after uploading.
  • Choosing High quality by default. It nearly tripled the size in our test (618.4 KB against 227.5 KB). Use it only when small text is hard to read at Balanced.
  • Mixing a photo of a page with a screenshot of it. Screenshots carry phone status bars and odd proportions. Photograph or scan the paper itself.
  • Uploading without opening the PDF. Open it once on your phone or computer and scroll through every page.

When not to use this tool

  • The document is already a PDF. Use the PDF compressor instead; turning a PDF into images and back loses text quality.
  • The PDF must stay searchable or digitally signed. A PDF built from photos contains pictures of text, not text, and a document's digital signature does not survive being photographed. Upload the original file you received.
  • The portal asks for separate files. Some forms have one upload slot per certificate, with a format and size given for each; the NEET (UG) 2026 bulletin, for example, sets 50 to 300 KB PDFs for certificates (Information Bulletin, page 16). Follow the slots, not one combined file.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I combine several certificates into one PDF?

Add all the photos or scans at once, put them in the order the portal asks for by dragging the handles or using the arrows, choose the page size, then select Create PDF. Each image becomes one page.

Are my documents uploaded to a server?

No. The PDF is built in your browser on your device. Your mark sheets, certificates and identity documents are not sent to ApplyReady or anyone else.

Which page size should I choose?

A4 suits most uploads and prints cleanly. Choose Same as each image when the portal wants the page to match the photo's own proportions, and follow the portal's instructions if they name a size.

Will landscape images be cropped?

No. With Orientation set to Automatic, each page follows its image, so a wide photo gets a landscape page. Rotate any page that was photographed sideways before creating the PDF.

Which image formats can I add?

JPEG, PNG and WebP images. iPhone HEIC photos need to be shared or saved as JPEG first.

The PDF is larger than the portal allows. What now?

Choose Compact quality and create the PDF again, or run it through the PDF compressor with the portal's limit. In our test, a two-page PDF went from 227.5 KB at Balanced to 136.3 KB at Compact, and to 77.3 KB with the compressor set to 100 KB.
