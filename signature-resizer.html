How to prepare a signature image

  1. Sign on plain white paper. Use a dark pen, black or blue, and your normal signature, a little larger than usual. Unlined paper gives the cleanest result.
  2. Photograph it flat. In daylight, from straight above, without your shadow across the page. If the paper looks grey or yellow in the photo, run it through the signature cleaner first.
  3. Crop close to the ink. Add the photo here and drag it so the signature fills the frame with a small margin on every side. A signature with a lot of empty paper around it becomes tiny once it is shrunk to 300 × 100 px.
  4. Resize and check. The result shows the requirement beside your file, with the size in KB and exact bytes and the pixel size read back from the file. Download when every row has a tick.

Reading the result

Signatures are mostly white paper, so they compress well: 300 × 100 px often comes to well under 30 KB at high quality. If your form gives a minimum as well, for example 10 to 20 KB, set it under Size, file limit and format, and a small JPEG is padded with empty bytes to reach it. If thin strokes look broken in the preview, crop closer so the signature is larger before it is reduced.

A picture of your signature is not a digital signature certificate; the guide to signature images and digital signatures explains the difference. For forms that ask for a 20 KB file, the resize to 20 KB page has that limit ready. Preparing signatures for many applicants? The batch image processor has a signature preset that pads each one to the exact size without cutting off any ink. If a form wants PNG instead of JPEG, the image converter changes the format, and the passport photo resizer prepares the photo that usually goes with the signature.

Questions

What size should a signature image be?

Each portal sets its own size. 300 × 100 px under 30 KB is common and is selected here. Follow the numbers in your form’s instructions; choose Custom size to enter different ones.

Should the background be white or transparent?

White, for application forms. Most portals ask for JPEG, which cannot be transparent. A transparent PNG is useful only for placing your signature on your own documents.

My signature looks faint after resizing. What can I do?

Crop closer, so the ink fills most of the frame before it is reduced. If the paper looked grey, clean the photo first with the signature cleaner, which darkens the ink and whitens the paper.

Is my signature uploaded anywhere?

No. It is cropped and compressed by your browser on this device. ApplyReady has no server that receives files.

Can I resize many signatures at once?

Yes, with the batch image processor and its signature preset. It fits each signature inside 300 × 100 px and fills the rest with white, so no ink is cut off, then lets you download them all as one ZIP file.

Your images are processed in this browser and never leave your device. Nothing is saved after you close the tab.
