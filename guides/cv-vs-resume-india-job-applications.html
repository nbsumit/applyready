CV, Resume or Bio-data: Which One Does the Employer Want?

Recruiters in India often say "CV" and mean a short resume. Academic posts mean something longer. Here is how to tell, and what each document contains.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 10 October 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

Same word, different documents

In India "resume", "CV" and "bio-data" are often used for the same thing, and a recruiter who writes "please share your CV" for a private-sector job usually wants a one- or two-page resume. The UK's National Careers Service, for example, uses "CV" for exactly that kind of short document (How to write a CV). In academic hiring, "CV" means something longer. Knowing which one is meant saves sending the wrong document.

The resume

A resume is selective: it shows the education, skills and results that matter for one job, and leaves out the rest. For students, freshers and most early-career roles it fits on one page; one page or two explains when it should not. It is tailored for each application, which tailoring a resume to a job description covers.

The academic or research CV

An academic CV is a complete record rather than a selection: education, research, publications with full citations, conference presentations, teaching, grants and fellowships, and referees. It is used for faculty and research positions, fellowships and many postgraduate applications, and it grows with your career. Follow any format the institution specifies; some ask for a particular order or page limit. The builder's Academic / Research CV template adds sections for publications and research.

The bio-data

A bio-data is an older format that leads with personal details such as date of birth, parents' names, address and languages. Some employers and institutions still provide their own bio-data or application format; when they do, fill in theirs. For a private-sector application that asks for a resume or CV, send a resume instead, and leave out personal details the job does not need.

How to tell which one is wanted

If the request says or showsSend
"CV" or "resume" for a company jobA one- or two-page resume
A faculty, research or fellowship postAn academic CV, in any format the notice gives
A form or template of its ownFill in that form
"Bio-data" with a list of fieldsThe fields asked for, in the order given

When in doubt, ask, or send the resume and offer a full CV on request. The resume builder has templates for both: Campus Placement / Fresher and Classic Professional for resumes, Academic / Research CV for research applications. If you are emailing it, see writing a short application email.
