What to Prepare Before You Start an Online Application

Some uploads cannot be corrected after you submit. Prepare and test every file against the notice first, and filling in the form becomes the easy part.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 11 October 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

Why the files come first

Most of an online application is typing; most of the trouble is files. A photo 12 KB over the limit, a certificate saved under the wrong name or a signature in capital letters can each stop the form. Some of these cannot be corrected later: NEET (UG) 2026 warns that "the facility for correction will not be given" for the photograph, signature and fingerprints (Information Bulletin, p. 16), and UPSC allows "no correction /alteration/modification in any field(s) of the Application Form" after submission (CSE 2026 notice, p. 2). Preparing and testing every file before you open the form avoids both problems.

Step 1: Read the notice and make a table

Open the official notification and the portal's instruction page. For every upload, write down the document, the format, the minimum and maximum size, the pixel or page size, and any file-name rule. Fill the table from the notice, not from memory or last year's figures: limits change. NEET (UG) raised its signature limit from 50 KB to 100 KB between 2025 and 2026, and SSC stopped taking an uploaded photo in 2024.

As an example of how much a notice can ask for, IBPS lists a photo, a signature, a left thumb impression, a handwritten declaration and a 10th certificate, each with its own size, and says the application "will not be registered" without them (CRP PO/MT-XVI, Annexure III). The IBPS page shows that table filled in.

Step 2: Prepare each file

  • Photograph: recent, plain background, face filling most of the frame. See taking a passport-size photo at home. Some exams also capture a live photo during the application, so check your camera works.
  • Signature: black ink on plain white paper, as the notice describes. See signature images for online forms.
  • Certificates: each page flat and readable, then one PDF per document unless the form asks for one file. See combining certificates into one PDF.
  • Sizes: use the resizer for images and compress PDF for documents over the limit.

Step 3: Name, test and organise

  • Rename each file as the instructions require. UPSC requires exact names such as photo, signature and board_certificate (UPSC: Instructions for uploading documents). Elsewhere, use letters, numbers and underscores only.
  • Check each file's type, size and pixel size against your table. The resizer's result shows the exact byte count.
  • Open every file once on a computer: nothing rotated, cut off or blank.
  • Check that your name and date of birth match across documents. A difference, for example after a name change, may need a supporting document; UPSC has a separate upload for a name-change affidavit or gazette.
  • Keep two folders: the untouched originals, and the final files for this form.

Step 4: Apply, and save the proof

  • Apply through the address printed in the notice. SSC, for instance, now takes applications only on ssc.gov.in or its mySSC app, and an OTR made on the old ssc.nic.in "will not be functional" (CGL 2026 notice, para 9.2).
  • Upload one file at a time and look at the preview. IBPS says the preview is there so an unclear image "may be re-uploaded".
  • Read the preview page before submitting, especially name, date of birth and category.
  • Save the confirmation page as a PDF and note the registration number.
  • Apply before the last day. Portals can be slow near the deadline, and a problem found then has no time to be fixed.

Keep a reusable pack

Keep the originals and the tested files together after you apply. The next form will have different limits, but resizing a good original takes a minute, while retaking a photo or rescanning a certificate takes much longer. Replace the photo when a notice sets a date rule, such as NEET (UG)'s "taken after 01 January 2026".
