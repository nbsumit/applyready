Application preparation

What to Prepare Before You Start an Online Application

Most of an online application is typing; most of the trouble is files. Prepare and test every file first, and filling in the form becomes the easy part.

ApplyReady guide | Published 11 October 2026 | Last updated 18 November 2026

Why the files come first

A resume PDF that is 300 KB over the limit, a certificate saved under a meaningless name or a photo that is too dark can each stop an application, or make it look careless to the person who opens it. Many job portals and university application forms time out after a period of inactivity, and some do not let you edit an application once it is submitted. Preparing and testing every file before you open the form avoids both problems, and it means the same set of files can be reused for the next application.

Step 1: Read the form and make a table

Open the job advert, the portal's upload instructions and any help page. For every upload, write down the document, the format, the minimum and maximum size, the pixel or page size, and any file-name rule. Fill the table from the form in front of you, not from memory or from another employer's portal: limits differ from one portal to the next, and they change.

An illustrative example: a portal that asks for a resume PDF under 2 MB, a 350 × 450 px photo under 50 KB, a signature under 20 KB and each certificate as a PDF under 500 KB. Your form will have its own numbers; the point is to have them all in one place before you start.

Step 2: Prepare each file

  • Resume: export it as a text-based PDF, not a scan or a photo, so that applicant tracking systems can read it. The resume builder exports one, and what makes a resume ATS-friendly explains why the format matters. Keep an editable copy too, because some portals ask for a Word file.
  • Photograph: recent, plain background, face filling most of the frame. See taking a passport-style photo at home.
  • Signature: dark ink on plain white paper, cleaned and cropped. See signature images for online forms.
  • ID and certificates: each page flat and readable, then one PDF per document unless the form asks for a single file. See combining documents into one PDF and phone camera tips for document scans.
  • Sizes: use the resizer for images and compress PDF for documents over the limit.

Step 3: Name, test and organise

  • Rename each file as the instructions require. Where the form gives no rule, use your name and the document, with letters, numbers and hyphens only, for example priya-sharma-resume.pdf or priya-sharma-degree-certificate.pdf. A clear name also helps the person who downloads many files called resume.pdf.
  • Check each file's type, size and pixel size against your table. The resizer's result shows the exact byte count.
  • Open every file once on a computer: nothing rotated, cut off or blank, and every page of a PDF in the right order.
  • Check that your name and date of birth match across documents. A difference, for example after a name change, may need a supporting document, such as a gazette notification or an affidavit.
  • Remove anything the employer does not need. An ID proof upload rarely needs both sides of every card, and a resume never needs your full ID number.
  • Keep two folders: the untouched originals, and the final files for this application.

Step 4: Apply, and save the proof

  • Apply through the employer's own careers page or a job portal you trust. Check the address before you enter personal details; staying safe with document uploads lists the warning signs of a fake job advert.
  • Upload one file at a time and look at the preview if the portal shows one.
  • Read the summary page before submitting, especially your name, email address, phone number and dates.
  • Save the confirmation page or email as a PDF and note any application or reference number.
  • Apply before the closing date. Portals can be slow near a deadline, and a problem found then has no time to be fixed.

Keep a reusable pack

Keep the originals and the tested files together after you apply. The next form will have different limits, but resizing a good original takes a minute, while retaking a photo or rescanning a certificate takes much longer. Update the resume for each role, as described in tailoring a resume to a job description, and replace the photo once it is more than a year or so old.
