How to Write a Resume Summary as a Fresher, with Examples

Two or three lines under your name that say which role you want and what evidence follows. Here is a structure and examples to adapt.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 10 October 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

What the summary is for

The summary is the two or three lines under your name. The National Careers Service describes it as "a few short lines that sum up who you are and what you hope to do", placed "just under your name and contact details" (How to write a CV). For a fresher, its job is to tell a reader within seconds which role you want and what evidence follows.

It replaces the older "Career Objective" ("to secure a challenging position in a reputed organisation..."), which says nothing that is specific to you. A summary that names the role, a skill and a piece of evidence gives the reader a reason to keep going.

A three-line structure

  1. Who you are and the role you want: your degree or current position, the role, and two or three skills that the advert asks for.
  2. Your strongest evidence: one project, internship or achievement, with a result if you have one.
  3. What you want to do next: the kind of work you want to grow into, in the employer's terms.

About 40 to 60 words is enough. Anything longer pushes your education and projects down the page.

Examples by background

These are illustrative. The projects and details are invented to show the structure; write yours from your own work.

Computer science graduate

"Computer science graduate applying for software trainee roles; Java, SQL and React. Built a college event registration site that the cultural committee used for its annual fest. Keen to work on web applications and learn code review and testing practices."

Electronics graduate

"ECE graduate looking for embedded systems roles; Embedded C, microcontrollers and I2C/SPI. Built a soil-moisture monitoring prototype that reports readings over Wi-Fi for my final-year project. Interested in firmware development and hardware testing."

Commerce graduate

"B.Com graduate applying for accounts trainee roles; Tally, GST basics and Excel pivot tables. Reconciled three months of bank statements for a family business during my internship. Looking to grow in month-end closing and statutory compliance."

Changing field

"Mechanical engineering graduate moving into data analysis; Python, SQL and Excel. Analysed a year of workshop maintenance logs to find the machines with the most downtime, as a self-directed project. Looking for a junior analyst role in operations or manufacturing."

Words to replace

Instead ofWrite
"Hardworking and dedicated"What you did that shows it, such as a project finished alongside exams
"Passionate self-starter"Something you built or learned on your own
"Good communication skills"Where you used them: a presentation, a team you coordinated
"Seeking a challenging role"The actual role, named as in the advert

Tailor it for each application

The summary is the quickest part to tailor: change the role name and swap the skills to the ones the advert stresses. Tailoring a resume to a job description covers the rest of the page. In the resume builder, the summary sits under your contact details in every template, and the preview shows how many lines it takes. The bullets that back up the summary are covered in bullet points that show results.
