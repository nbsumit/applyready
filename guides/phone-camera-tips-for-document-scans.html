How to Scan Documents with a Phone Camera So the Text Stays Readable

Software can shrink a sharp page but cannot repair a blurred or shadowed one. Get the light and the angle right, then check the smallest text before uploading.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 11 October 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

Why the photo matters more than the software

A compressor can make a sharp, evenly lit page smaller and still readable. It cannot recover text that was blurred, shadowed or lost in glare when the photo was taken. Portals that check documents say so directly: IBPS warns that if a document is "unclear / smudged the candidate's application may be rejected" (CRP PO/MT-XVI notification, Annexure III). This page is about getting the capture right; size limits come after.

Light

  • Use daylight from a window, falling on the page from the side or the front.
  • Check that neither you nor the phone casts a shadow on the page. When the light is behind you, the phone's shadow lands exactly where you are pointing it.
  • Tilt glossy or laminated documents slightly until any glare spot moves off the text. A glare spot hides text completely.
  • Leave the flash off for flat pages. It produces a bright centre and dark corners.

Angle, distance and focus

  • Hold the phone level, directly above the page. If the page looks like a trapezoid on screen, the far edge will be smaller and softer.
  • Fill the frame with the page and a thin margin. Move closer rather than zooming; digital zoom only enlarges pixels.
  • Tap the text to focus, wait a moment, and rest your elbows on the table to keep still. Take two or three shots and keep the sharpest.
  • One page per photo. Each page becomes its own image or its own PDF page.

What official scanning instructions ask for

Where an authority describes how documents should be scanned, it usually gives a resolution, a colour mode and a format. These are the ones we have checked:

AuthorityWhat it says
IBPS (bank recruitment)"Set the scanner resolution to a minimum of 200 dpi"; "Set Colour to True Colour"; crop "to the edge of the photograph/signature/ left thumb impression / hand written declaration"; the 10th certificate as a PDF on A4 pages, up to 500 KB
PAN, DSC-based online applicationSupporting documents at 200 DPI, black and white, as "PDF/A or JPEG", maximum 300 KB per page (Protean guidelines)
NEET (UG) 2026Certificates as PDF files between 50 and 300 KB (Information Bulletin, p. 16)
UPSCCertificates as PDF, 50 to 300 KB, each with a set file name (UPSC: Instructions for uploading documents)

A phone photo of a page is usually far more than 200 DPI across an A4 sheet, so resolution is rarely the problem; light and focus are. If a form wants black and white, convert at the end, not at capture time, so you can still read faint stamps in the colour original.

Scanner modes in phone apps

Many phones have a document mode that finds the page edges and straightens them. It is useful, but check its "enhance" or "black and white" filter: strong filters can turn a stamp or a signature into a solid blob and remove the colour from seals. If the colours matter, turn the filter off.

After capture

  1. Zoom to 100% and read the smallest text on the page: a roll number, a date, a percentage. If you cannot read it, retake the photo.
  2. Name files by content and order, for example 01-class10-certificate.jpg, and keep the originals in a separate folder.
  3. Put pages in order in one PDF with image to PDF; combining documents into one PDF covers page order and quality settings.
  4. If the PDF is over the limit, see compressing a PDF for upload portals.

For a signature rather than a page, the same light rules apply, and the signature cleaner removes the grey from paper photographed indoors.

Frequently asked questions

Why does white paper look grey?

The camera exposed for an average scene. Tap the paper and raise the exposure slightly, and use daylight.

Is a scanner app better than the camera?

Either works when the light is even and the page is flat. The app saves time on cropping and straightening; check its filters.

How do I handle a bound booklet?

Flatten the page under a sheet of clean glass, or photograph from a little farther away and crop. Strong curves near the spine distort text.
