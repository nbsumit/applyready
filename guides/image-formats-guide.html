JPEG, PNG or WebP: Which Image Format Should You Upload?

For exam and job portals the answer is almost always JPEG. Here is why, with the same photo measured in all three formats.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 30 September 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

The short answer

Upload JPEG unless the form names another format. Every exam notice we have checked asks for JPG or JPEG for photos and signatures: SSC, UPSC, IBPS and NEET (UG) all do, and the online PAN application does too. Use PNG only for a signature with a transparent background that you will place on top of another document, and WebP only where a form lists it.

What the three formats are for

JPEG is lossy: it discards detail the eye is less likely to notice, and the quality setting controls how much. The standard dates from 1992 (JPEG committee). MDN describes it as "particularly useful for photographs" and warns that lossy compression on content "requiring sharpness, like diagrams or charts, can produce unsatisfactory results" (MDN image format guide).

PNG is lossless: it stores every pixel exactly (W3C PNG specification). It suits screenshots, line drawings and anything that needs transparency, because "JPEG has no alpha channel" (MDN). It has no quality setting, so it cannot be squeezed to a KB limit.

WebP supports both lossy and lossless compression and transparency. Google reports lossy WebP files 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEGs (Google WebP); MDN gives 25–35%.

What the difference looks like in kilobytes

The PNG is more than seven times the JPEG because it keeps every variation in skin and hair exactly. The resizer reports this instead of producing an oversized file: asked for a PNG under 20 KB at 350 × 450 px, it says the PNG needs 196 KB. Signatures show the same pattern on a smaller scale: our 300 × 100 px test signature was 8.0 KB as JPEG and 37.8 KB as PNG.

WebP was the smallest, but none of the exam notices we checked accept it, so the saving rarely helps on a government form. Where a limit is tight and only JPEG is allowed, reduce the pixel size instead; see getting a photo under a KB limit without blur.

Converting between formats

Renaming a file does not convert it. A PNG renamed to .jpg is still a PNG inside, and a portal that reads the file's first bytes will reject it. Open the image in the resizer and choose JPEG as the output format: the browser re-encodes it from the pixels.

When a transparent image becomes a JPEG, the transparent areas need a colour. The resizer fills them with the colour set under Background fill, white by default, because "JPEG cannot be transparent". Phone photos in HEIC format can only be opened if your browser can decode them; if the resizer says it cannot read the file, export the photo as JPEG on the phone first, or set the camera to save JPEG.

Frequently asked questions

Is JPG different from JPEG?

No. They are two extensions for the same format.

Why can't I lower the quality of a PNG?

Because PNG is lossless. Browsers ignore a quality value for PNG, so only fewer pixels make it smaller.

Can I upload a PDF instead of a photo?

Only where the form asks for a PDF. Photo and signature fields on exam portals ask for images.
