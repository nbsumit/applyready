Taking a Passport-Style Photo at Home: Light, Background and Framing

Most rejected photos fail on light and background, not on file size. Here is how to take one that passes the first time.

By Sumit Sengar | Last reviewed October 2026 | 5 min read

What the checker is really looking at

Whether the check is done by software or by a person, the same few things decide the result: is the face fully visible and evenly lit, is the background plain and light, is the head the right size in the frame, and is the picture sharp. A phone can meet all four if you control the light and the distance.

Always check the form's own instructions. Background colour, head position and clothing rules differ between portals and between exams. Treat this guide as a method, and follow your form's written requirements where they differ.

Light: use a window, not the room light

  • Stand facing a window with daylight coming in, in the morning or afternoon. Avoid direct sun on your face, which creates squinting and hard shadows.
  • Turn off ceiling lights that sit above you. They cast shadows under the eyebrows and nose.
  • If one side of your face is darker, hold a white sheet of paper or a white cloth on that side, just outside the frame, to bounce light back.
  • Do not use the phone's flash. It flattens the face and leaves a bright spot on the forehead.

Background: plain and a step behind you

A white or very light wall works well. Stand about a metre in front of it so your shadow does not fall on the wall. If you do not have a clear wall, hang a plain light sheet. Check the edges of the frame: curtains, photo frames and switches near the corners are easy to miss.

Framing and distance

  • Camera height: at eye level. A camera held low makes the chin look large; a camera held high shortens the face.
  • Distance: about an arm and a half away with the back camera, or the phone on a stack of books and the timer set to 10 seconds. The back camera gives a sharper image than the selfie camera.
  • Zoom: do not use digital zoom. Take the photo wider and crop afterwards; zoom reduces sharpness.
  • Head position: face the camera straight on, shoulders level, neutral expression, mouth closed, eyes open.
  • Space: leave room above the head and around the shoulders so you can crop to the ratio the form needs.

Take at least five photos and pick the sharpest. Look at each at full size before you choose, because small blur is hard to see on the phone screen.

Things that cause rejection

  • Beauty filters or auto retouching in the camera app. Turn them off in the camera settings.
  • Glasses with glare. Tilt the glasses slightly down, or remove them if the form allows.
  • A cap, a scarf over the forehead or a heavy shadow across one eye.
  • A photo of a photo, or a screenshot. Always upload the original camera file.
  • A very old photo. Many forms require a recent one, so check the rule on your form.

After the photo: crop, resize, then print if needed

Crop to the ratio the form uses, then resize to its dimensions and size limit. The 50 KB guide shows the order that keeps a face sharp. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a studio to get a photo accepted online?

Not usually. Many online forms check size, background, framing and clarity, which a phone can meet with window light and a plain wall. Some physical documents, such as a passport, have stricter photograph rules and may require a studio photo; check the instructions for the document you are applying for.

Which camera should I use, front or back?

The back camera, because it has a better sensor and lens. Use a timer or a helper so you can stand at a distance. If you must use the front camera, wipe the lens and turn off any beauty mode.

Can I wear glasses?

It depends on the form. If glasses are allowed, avoid glare on the lenses and make sure the frames do not cover your eyes. If you normally wear glasses and the form says "without glasses", follow the form.

How recent should the photo be?

Many forms ask for a photo taken within the last few months, and some print the date on the photo. Check the notice. A recent photo also avoids questions at document verification.

Can I change the background in an app?

Editing the background is risky. Soft edges around hair and ears are easy for a checker or a person to spot, and some forms prohibit edited photos. A clean wall is safer.

How many photos should I take?

At least five. Choose the sharpest one at full zoom, and keep the other good ones in case a form asks for a different crop.
