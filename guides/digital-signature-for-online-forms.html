Signatures

Signature Images for Online Forms, and How They Differ from a Digital Signature

"Upload signature" means a picture of your handwritten signature. Here is how to make one that is clean, readable and the right size.

ApplyReady guide | Published 11 October 2026 | Last updated 18 November 2026

A signature image is not a digital signature

Job portals, university application forms and onboarding forms often ask you to "upload signature": a picture of your handwritten signature. A digital signature is something else. The Controller of Certifying Authorities explains that a Digital Signature Certificate is issued by a licensed Certifying Authority (CCA: How to get a DSC), and that there is no "specimen" digital signature, because "The Digital signature changes with content of the message" (CCA: Digital Signature). Electronic signatures are recognised under Section 5 of the Information Technology Act, 2000, and eSign is "an online electronic signature service" for signing a document (CCA: eSign).

You need a DSC only for filings that ask for one. For an application form that says "upload signature", or an offer letter you are asked to sign and return as a scan, you need an image, and the rest of this page is about making a good one.

What application forms usually ask for

Each form sets its own rule, so read the instructions on the form you are filling. Most of them describe the same kind of file, and the table shows the typical shape of those rules with clearly illustrative numbers.

SettingTypical request
FormatJPEG or JPG; PNG only when the form lists it
File sizeA range or a maximum, for example 10 to 20 KB, or under 50 KB
Pixel sizeA wide strip, for example 300 × 100 px or 140 × 60 px
Ink and paperDark ink, usually black, on plain white paper
StyleYour normal running signature, not your name in capital letters

One rule applies everywhere: the signature you upload should be the one you sign on paper. If you later sign an offer letter, a joining form or an attendance sheet, a recruiter may compare the two, so keep one reference signature and use it on every form.

Paper, pen and photo

  • Paper: plain white, unlined. Ruled lines and creases turn into dark streaks.
  • Pen: black ink with a medium tip. Faint strokes break up when the image is reduced to a small size, and blue ink can turn grey after cleaning.
  • Size on paper: sign large. Draw a light pencil box about the shape of the upload (for example 6 cm wide and 2 cm high), sign inside it so the signature fills most of it, and crop to the box later.
  • Photo: daylight from the side, phone held level above the paper, no shadow across it. See phone camera tips for document scans.
  • Scanner: if you have a flatbed scanner, scan at 200 or 300 DPI in colour or greyscale. A scan is flatter and more even than a phone photo, but the cleaning step below still helps.

Clean, crop and size

A phone photo of white paper is grey or yellow. The signature cleaner turns the paper white and the ink dark with two sliders: raise paper whiteness until the background is clean, and lower it again if thin strokes start to vanish; raise ink darkness for faint strokes. Check the thinnest stroke at full size. The cleaner can also export a PNG with a transparent background for placing a signature on a document you are editing.

Cleaning also makes the file smaller. Our 1600 × 900 px test photo came out of the cleaner as a 46.7 KB JPEG. Resized to 300 × 100 px it was 8.0 KB before cleaning and 6.3 KB after; at 709 × 236 px it needed quality 0.89 to fit 20 KB uncleaned, but fitted at 0.95 once cleaned (17.5 KB).

Then open the cleaned file in the resizer, enter the size the form asks for, and crop close to the ink. If the form gives a size in centimetres, switch the unit to cm and enter the DPI it states; pixels, cm, DPI and KB explains the conversion. A small signature often comes out under a minimum: our 140 × 60 px version was 3.1 KB against a 10 KB minimum, and the resizer padded it without changing the picture. The resize to 20 KB page covers the common signature limit in more detail.

Common mistakes

  • Signing your name in capital letters instead of your normal signature.
  • A tiny signature in a large white image. Crop close to the ink so the signature fills its box.
  • Saving as PNG when the form asks for JPEG.
  • Stretching a wide signature into a square box. Crop to the target shape instead.
  • A different signature on each form. Keep one reference signature and use it everywhere.
  • Photographing the signature at an angle, which makes it look slanted and uneven.
