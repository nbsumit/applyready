How to Make a Clean Signature Image for Online Forms

A signature file is rejected for being grey, crooked, too faint or too large. Almost all of that is fixed before you open any software.

By Sumit Sengar | Last reviewed October 2026 | 5 min read

Start with paper, not software

The best signature image is a good photo of a good signature. Spend two minutes on the setup and the software step becomes easy.

  • Paper: plain white, unlined, unwrinkled. Lines and creases become dark streaks after processing and can be mistaken for part of the signature.
  • Pen: black or dark blue ink, a ballpoint or gel pen with a medium tip. Pencils and light-coloured pens fade after compression, and thick markers fill in loops.
  • Signature: sign the way you sign on your documents. The form usually asks for a signature that matches your records, so do not draw a neater version just for the upload.
  • Space: leave a clear margin around the signature. You will crop it later, and a margin makes cropping easier.

Photograph it so the background is even

Place the paper on a flat table near a window in daylight. Stand so your own shadow does not fall across the page, and hold the phone directly above the paper rather than at an angle. An angled photo makes one side of the paper darker and distorts the signature, which cannot be fully fixed afterwards.

If the paper looks yellow or grey in the photo, that is not a problem for the next step. What matters is that the lighting is even: a gentle gradient is easy to remove, while a hard shadow edge is not.

For more on camera settings, see scanning documents with a phone camera.

Clean the background and crop

A cleaned signature has a pure white background and dark ink. In the ApplyReady signature cleaner two sliders do the work:

  • Paper whiteness: raise it if the paper still looks grey or yellow; lower it if thin strokes of your signature start to disappear.
  • Ink darkness: raise it to strengthen faint strokes; lower it if the signature starts to look heavy or loops fill in.

Move the sliders while watching the preview at full size, not the small thumbnail. The right setting is the one where the thinnest stroke of the signature is still continuous. Then crop close to the signature, leaving a small even margin on every side.

Check your thinnest stroke. Zoom to 100 percent and follow the end of the signature with your eye. If the line breaks into dots, lower the whiteness setting a little.

Get the size right

Signature fields usually have both a pixel size and a small file-size limit, such as 10 to 20 KB. Signatures compress very well because most of the image is flat white. A signature of 400 x 200 pixels is 80,000 pixels, so even a 10 KB file gives about 1 bit per pixel, which is plenty for an image made of white paper and a few dark strokes.

Field requirementWhat to do
Size in pixels given (for example 400 x 200)Set exactly that. Do not let the tool keep the original ratio if it differs; crop to the ratio first.
Size in centimetres given (for example 4 x 2 cm)Convert with the DPI guide. At 300 DPI, 4 x 2 cm is about 472 x 236 pixels.

Common mistakes

  • Signing on a surface with a visible texture, such as a notebook with ruled lines.
  • Photographing at an angle, so the signature is narrower at one end.
  • Saving the result as PNG when the form asks for JPEG. PNG files are larger and are often refused.
  • Letting the tool stretch a wide signature into a square box.
  • Using different signatures on different forms. Keep one reference signature and follow it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a digital signature certificate for these forms?

No. A signature image for a form is a picture of your handwritten signature. It is a different thing from a digital signature certificate (DSC), which is a cryptographic credential issued by an authority and used for specific filings. Follow the form: if it says "upload signature", it wants an image.

Can I draw my signature on a phone screen?

Some forms accept it, but a handwritten signature on paper usually looks more natural and matches your documents better. If the instructions say the signature must be on white paper, use paper.

What if my signature is rejected for being too light?

Re-sign with a darker pen, raise the ink darkness in the cleaner and re-check that thin strokes are still continuous. Avoid pencil and light blue pens.

Should I sign in capitals?

Sign the way you sign on your bank records and identity documents. Some forms say explicitly whether capital letters are allowed. Read the instruction for your form rather than assuming.

Which format should the file be?

The form will say. If it does not, JPEG is the safest choice for portals; PNG can be larger and is sometimes refused. Keep a transparent-background PNG only if the form explicitly asks for it.

Can I reuse the same signature file for several applications?

Usually yes, as long as each form accepts its size and format. Keep the cleaned file in your application folder, and resize a copy when a form has different dimensions.
