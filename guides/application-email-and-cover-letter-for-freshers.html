Writing a Short Application Email or Cover Letter as a Fresher

Four short paragraphs, one clear request, one attachment named properly. That is enough.

By Sumit Sengar | Last reviewed October 2026 | 4 min read

Email or cover letter: which one?

If the portal has a field for a cover letter, paste a short version there. If you are applying by email, the email body is your cover letter: do not attach a separate letter and leave the email empty, because the reader may never open the attachment. Either way, keep it short enough to read on a phone.

The subject line

A clear subject helps the right person find your email. Use a formula: Application for [Role], [Your Name], plus a reference number if the advert gives one.

  • Application for Junior Accountant, Priya Sharma
  • Application: Software Trainee (Ref ST-24), Rahul Verma

Avoid subjects such as "Job", "Resume", "Urgent", or all capitals.

Four paragraphs

  1. Who you are and what you are applying for. One sentence: your course or current role and the position. Mention where you saw the advert.
  2. Why you fit. Two or three sentences linking one or two real experiences or projects to what the advert asks for. Use the employer's own terms where they are true.
  3. Why this employer. One or two sentences showing you know what the company does. Be specific; a generic compliment is worse than none.
  4. The ask and the attachment. Say that your resume is attached, that you would welcome the chance to discuss the role, and thank the reader.
Length: about 120 to 180 words. If it needs scrolling on a phone, trim it.

Example for a fresher

Subject: Application for Graduate Trainee (Operations), Anita Rao

Dear Hiring Team,

I am a final-year B.Com student at [College], and I am writing to apply for the Graduate Trainee (Operations) position I saw on [website].

In my internship at a local distribution firm I tracked daily stock for about 120 items in a shared spreadsheet and reduced out-of-stock days from six to two a month. I also coordinated delivery schedules with three suppliers, which matches the vendor coordination mentioned in your advert.

I am drawn to [Company] because of its work in [specific area], and I would like to learn the operations process from the ground up.

My resume is attached. I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can contribute to your team. Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,
Anita Rao
Phone: [number] | Email: [email]

The figures above are an example; use only facts that are true for you.

Attachment and file name

  • Attach one resume, as a PDF, unless the advert asks for another format. A PDF keeps the layout the same on every device. If the employer asks for Word, export a .docx instead.
  • Name the file Firstname_Lastname_Role.pdf, for example Anita_Rao_Operations.pdf. Avoid "resume_final_v3".
  • Check the file size is modest, typically well under a few MB, so it is not blocked.
  • Send the email to yourself first and open the attachment to confirm it is the right file.

Before you press send

  • Check the recipient's name and company are spelled correctly.
  • Check there are no leftovers from a previous application, such as another company's name.
  • Read it once aloud. If a sentence is hard to say, it is hard to read.
  • Use a professional email address, ideally based on your name.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a cover letter if the portal does not ask?

No. If the portal has no field for it, a good resume is enough. If you are emailing a recruiter directly, the email body serves as the cover letter.

Should I attach the resume as PDF or Word?

PDF unless the employer says otherwise, because it keeps the same layout on every device. If they ask for Word, export a .docx.

How long should I wait before following up?

About a week after the stated closing date, or ten days after applying if no date was given. Send one short, polite follow-up, then wait.

Can I send the same email to many companies?

Do not. Use the same structure but change the role, company and the sentence about why you fit. A mass email with the wrong company name is easy to spot.

Which email address should I use?

A simple one based on your name. Avoid nicknames and numbers that look unprofessional, and check it daily during your search.
