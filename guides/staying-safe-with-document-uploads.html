Privacy and safety

How to Check an Online Tool Before You Give It Your ID or Photo

A resizer does not need a copy of your Aadhaar. Two quick tests show whether a tool uploads your file, and a few habits keep documents safe.

By Sumit Sengar | Published 11 October 2026 | Last updated 24 October 2026

Why application documents need extra care

A photograph, a signature and an identity document together are much of what someone needs to impersonate you. Many free converters upload your file to their server, process it there and send back the result; what happens to the copy afterwards depends on the operator. For an identity document, choose tools that do not need to receive the file at all, and share documents only with the official portal.

Two tests for any tool

The airplane-mode test

  1. Open the tool and let the page finish loading.
  2. Switch on airplane mode, or turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data.
  3. Choose a file and run the tool.

If it still produces a result, your file was not uploaded: there was no connection to upload it through. If it shows a network error or waits forever, it was sending the file to a server.

The network check

On a computer, open the browser's developer tools (F12 in most browsers), go to the Network tab and run the tool. Uploading shows up as a request roughly the size of your file going to some server. A tool that runs on your device shows no such request.

All of ApplyReady's tools (the resizer, signature cleaner, image to PDF, compress PDF and the resume builder) pass both tests. You do not need to take our word for it; run the tests yourself.

What official advice says

CERT-In's safe-internet booklet, published through the Cyber Swachhta Kendra, covers the situations applicants meet most (CERT-In: Safe Internet Best Practices):

  • "Avoid filling of forms that comes via unsolicited email or clicking on links in emails received from untrusted sources."
  • "Emails or messages that create a sense of urgency is a warning!"
  • "Avoid sharing your personal information like address, mobile number, personal mail id and other sensitive identity related information on social media."
  • "Expand shortened URLs and verify them before clicking."

For job applications, the National Career Service adds a warning about fees: "NCS does not charge any fee at any stage of registration, job application, or interview processing. Jobseekers should not pay fees to employers." It asks users to verify information only on its official site and gives 1930 as the number for reporting cyber fraud (ncs.gov.in).

Habits that protect your documents

  • Apply only at the address in the notice. Fake application sites copy the look of real ones. SSC, for example, takes applications only on ssc.gov.in and in its mySSC app.
  • Send only what is asked for. Upload the pages the notice lists, not every document you have; the document checklist helps you list them. For Aadhaar and most passport applications you do not supply a photo file at all, as Aadhaar, PAN and passport photos explains.
  • Prefer issued digital copies where accepted. Under Rule 9A of the Digital Locker rules (notified 8 February 2017), documents issued into DigiLocker can be accepted "at par with the physical documents" (MeitY letter with the Gazette notification; DigiLocker). Sharing from DigiLocker avoids sending a scan around.
  • Use your own device. On a shared computer, delete downloaded files and clear the download list when you finish, and do not save drafts there.
  • Keep IDs out of group chats. Send them one to one, only to someone who needs them.

What ApplyReady stores

Images and PDFs are processed in your browser and are not sent to us. The resume builder keeps a draft in your browser's own storage only if you switch drafts on, and you can delete it from the same panel. The site itself is hosted on GitHub Pages, and Google may set advertising cookies where ads are shown; the privacy policy describes both and how to opt out. Never send us your documents by email: we do not need them to answer a question, as the contact page explains.

Frequently asked questions

Does the padlock in the address bar mean a site is safe?

It means the connection is encrypted. It says nothing about what the site does with a file once it arrives.

I uploaded an ID to a site I do not trust. What now?

Watch for unexpected messages and account activity, change passwords connected to that document, and use the site's deletion contact if it has one. Suspected fraud can be reported on 1930, the number NCS gives.

Is a resume as sensitive as an ID?

Less so, but it contains your phone number, email and history. Leave out details the job does not need, such as a full home address.
