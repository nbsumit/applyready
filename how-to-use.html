How to use ApplyReady

How the tools work, in short: what every tool has in common, then the image resizer and the resume builder step by step. For the exact clicks of one task, with screenshots, each section links to its tutorial.

What every tool has in common

  • Your files stay on your device. Every tool runs in your browser. Nothing you add is uploaded, so the tools also work on a slow connection and, after your first visit, offline.
  • Add, check, download. Add a file, check the preview, the page thumbnails or the result table, then download. Most PDF tools open the finished file again and check it before they offer the download.
  • Your files are not kept. Files you add are cleared when you close the tab. Resumes save in this browser as you type (on a shared computer, turn off Save on this device before you type), and the your data page shows and deletes everything ApplyReady keeps.

Every tool is listed on the all tools page, by category.

Resizing and compressing images

The image resizer sets an exact output size in pixels (for example 350 × 450 px) and a maximum file size (for example 50 KB). The step-by-step tutorials show the same steps with screenshots: resize a photo for an application form, compress an image to a KB limit, make and resize a signature image and process many photos at once.

  1. Choose what you are preparing: Pick the requirement from your form, such as ID / passport photo, Signature or Thumb impression, or pick Custom size to enter your own. Each option shows its pixel size and file limit. File-size pages such as resize to 20 KB and resize to 50 KB open with their limit already chosen; use Change to pick another. Check the requirements on the form you are applying to; a preset does not guarantee acceptance by a particular portal.
  2. Add your image: Drop a file into the workspace or select Choose image. You can also try a sample image, which suits the requirement you chose. Supported files are JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP and AVIF, up to 50 MB and 64 megapixels. Animated files produce a still image.
  3. Position it in the frame: Drag the image inside the frame, and use the rotation (±90°) and zoom buttons. The frame always has the shape of the output, so nothing is stretched.
  4. Change size, file limit or format only if needed: Open Size, file limit and format to edit width, height, units, the minimum and maximum KB, the file format (JPEG for photos, PNG for graphics, WebP for the web) and the crop shape. Preset values stay editable; editing one switches to Custom size. Use original size keeps the whole image at its own dimensions. Output is limited to 20–8,000 pixels on each side and 32 megapixels.
  5. Optional background and name strip: Under Format and crop, choose the Background fill for transparent areas when saving as JPEG, and switch on Add a name and date strip to print a name or date under the photo. The frame adjusts so the photo is not distorted.
  6. Resize and download: Select Resize and compress. The result shows the requirement next to your file (file size, dimensions and format), each one checked, with Download image first. If the limit cannot be met at your chosen dimensions, increase the limit, reduce the dimensions or try another format. ApplyReady does not silently shrink your output. Use Adjust again to change something, or Replace to start with another file.

The other tools work the same way, in your browser: clean a photographed signature with the signature cleaner before resizing it, combine scans into one PDF with image to PDF, bring a scanned PDF under a portal's limit with compress PDF, and lay out printed copies of a photo with passport photo print. For many files at once, the batch image processor applies one size and file limit to a whole list of photos or signatures and gives you a ZIP file; the image compressor and the image converter work the same way for making files smaller or changing their format. Before you upload anything, the file checker shows a file’s real type, exact size, pixel size or page count and hidden metadata such as a GPS location, and text to PDF turns a typed cover letter or statement into a clean PDF. If you prepare documents for other people, such as customers at a cyber cafe, applicant workspaces give each person a separate, temporary space with their own photo, signature and PDF actions and one ZIP, and finishing a person wipes their files from the tab.

The PDF tools follow the same pattern: add a file, check the preview or the page thumbnails, then download a copy that has been opened again and checked. The tutorial on how to merge PDF files and photos into one PDF shows it step by step. Join several files with Merge PDF, take pages out with Split PDF, reorder, turn or delete pages with Organise PDF pages, number them with Add page numbers, mark copies with Watermark PDF, save pages as pictures with PDF to images, and copy the words out with Extract text from PDF. A password-protected PDF can be opened if you know its password; it is used only in your browser tab.

Building a resume that software can read

The resume builder makes single-column resumes made of real text, which people and applicant tracking software can both read in order. No layout guarantees that a particular system reads a resume perfectly; will an applicant tracking system read your resume correctly? explains what does and does not matter. The tutorials show how to create a resume from blank, open, rename and edit a saved resume and back up and restore your resumes. If a job asks for a CV or a bio-data instead, read CV, resume or bio-data: which one does the employer want? In short:

  1. Start from a sample or a blank resume: select Try a sample to see a complete example, or start with empty fields.
  2. Fill in your contact details: enter your name, target title, email, phone, location and links. Separators appear only between the fields you fill in.
  3. Add experience, education and projects: use the Add buttons to add entries. Reorder them with the move up and move down buttons, or delete sections you do not need.
  4. Change skills and headings: enter your skills in up to four labelled rows. Rename any row label or section heading (for example Internships instead of Work Experience); headings you leave unchanged follow the selected template's wording.
  5. Choose a design: open Style to choose one of 15 single-column templates (filter by General, Students & freshers, Tech, Senior or Specialist roles), preview it, set A4 or US Letter paper, change typography and reorder sections. Your entered content is preserved when switching templates.
  6. Saving and several resumes: your resume saves in this browser as you type; the line under the title says when. Open My resumes to start another resume, duplicate one to tailor it for a different job, rename, delete, or export a backup file that you can import later or on another device. On a shared computer, turn off Save on this device in the Download step before you type. The your data page shows every resume saved in this browser and can back up, restore or delete them all at once.
  7. Compare with a job description: in the Review step, paste the job advert to see which of its key terms your resume already uses and which are missing. The comparison runs on your device and the advert is not saved. Add only terms that truly describe your experience.
  8. Review and download: open Review to see what is missing, with a Fix button that goes to the field, then open Download to export PDF, editable Word or plain text. For characters outside the direct PDF engine's supported fonts, ApplyReady opens browser printing to preserve your text; choose Save as PDF. Review the downloaded file before submitting it.
