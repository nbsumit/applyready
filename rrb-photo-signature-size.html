Why this page gives no RRB figures

Railway Recruitment Boards publish their photo and signature rules inside each Centralised Employment Notice (CEN), in the part that explains how to fill in the online application. Each recruitment has its own CEN, so the rules are read from the CEN for your post. The rules are not published once for all RRB recruitment.

When we checked this page on 24 October 2026, none of the RRB websites that host the CENs could be opened from our system, so we could not read a current annexure. Our editorial policy does not allow a file size, pixel size or date on this site that we have not read in an official document. This page therefore does not repeat the figures that circulate on other websites. Some of those figures disagree with each other, and some appear to come from older CENs.

Check the latest official notification. Open the detailed CEN for the post you are applying for on your RRB's official website, and use the photo and signature figures in its application instructions. The resizer below accepts any figure you enter.

If you have the current CEN and can tell us the section and page that gives the upload rules, please use Report an error above. We add a figure to this page only after reading it in the CEN itself.

Finding the upload rules in a CEN

  1. Start from your RRB's website. Each of the regional RRBs posts the CENs. Some are moving to a shared portal: the RRB Ranchi site, for example, now shows a notice that it is "Redirecting to RRB Ranchi" on "the new Railway Recruitment Board, Ranchi website" under rrb.indianrailways.gov.in. Older direct links to PDF files on regional sites may no longer work, so download the CEN from the site itself rather than from a saved link.
  2. Open the detailed CEN, not the short or indicative notice. The short notice announces the recruitment. The detailed CEN contains the eligibility rules and the application instructions.
  3. Search the PDF for "photograph" and "signature". Look in the instructions for filling in the online application, where the documents to upload are listed.
  4. Write down five things for each image: the file format, the minimum and maximum size in KB, the pixel or centimetre dimensions if any, the background colour, and any rule about the ink or the paper for the signature.
  5. Check for corrigenda. RRBs publish corrections to a CEN as separate notices. A corrigendum issued after the CEN overrides the figure in the CEN.

A CEN that gives a size in centimetres or millimetres but not in pixels can be entered directly: under Size, file limit and format, change Unit to centimetres and set the print DPI the CEN gives. If the CEN gives no DPI, our guide to pixels, cm and DPI explains how the conversion works and what to choose.

Entering the CEN's figures in the resizer

The tool on this page starts on Custom size because there is no RRB preset. The steps:

  1. Add your photo or signature, and drag it inside the frame so the face or the signature fills it.
  2. Open Size, file limit and format. Enter the width and height from the CEN, and the minimum and maximum file size in KB. If the CEN gives only a maximum, leave the minimum empty.
  3. Leave the format as JPEG unless the CEN asks for another one. JPEG is the format that shrinks by lowering quality; a PNG of the same image is often several times larger.
  4. Select Resize and compress, then compare the result with the CEN in the table labelled "Your file compared with the requirement". It shows the exact byte count as well as the KB figure.

The tool tries the highest quality first and lowers it only as far as the maximum requires. If the file comes out under the minimum, it pads the JPEG with comment data, so the image itself is unchanged. To see how far quality has to drop at different pixel sizes, read the measured tables on the 20 KB and 50 KB pages: a large image squeezed into a small limit looks worse than a small image at the size the form asks for.

Identity checks at RRB exams

The one RRB process we could verify from an official source is identity checking. A Ministry of Railways press release of 9 July 2025 says: "E-KYC based Aadhar authentication has been used to authenticate identity of candidates for the first time in such large-scale exams achieving more than 95% success" (PIB, Release ID 2143531). The release does not mention photo or signature file sizes.

An application photo is only useful if it can be matched with you. Use a recent photo that looks like you on the day of the exam, follow the CEN's background and framing rules, and keep printed copies of the same photo if the CEN asks you to bring them. Our guides on taking a passport photo at home and fixing common upload errors cover the parts that are the same for every exam.

Frequently asked questions: RRB

What is the RRB photo size?

It is set in each Centralised Employment Notice (CEN) and can differ between recruitments. Check the latest official notification: open the detailed CEN for your post on your RRB's official website and use the figures in its application instructions.

Why doesn't ApplyReady list the RRB photo and signature sizes?

We publish a figure only after reading it in an official document. On 24 October 2026 none of the RRB websites hosting the CENs could be opened from our system, so we could not check the current figures. This page will add them, with citations, once we can.

Where are the upload rules in an RRB CEN?

In the instructions for filling in the online application. Search the detailed CEN PDF for "photograph" and "signature", and check any corrigendum issued after it.

Can I use this tool for an RRB application?

Yes. Choose Custom size and enter the width, height and KB limits from your CEN. The tool keeps the highest quality that fits, pads a file that is under the minimum, and shows the exact byte count.

Does the RRB check my identity at the exam?

According to a Ministry of Railways press release of 9 July 2025, RRBs used e-KYC based Aadhaar authentication to verify candidates' identity. Use a recent photo that looks like you.
