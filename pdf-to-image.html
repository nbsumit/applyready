      Image

      Format

      JPG makes smaller files. PNG keeps text edges crisp but is larger.

      How it works

      Each page is drawn by your browser at the resolution you choose and saved as an image. DPI is the number of pixels for each inch of paper: an A4 page at 150 DPI is 1240 × 1754 pixels, which reads well on a screen; 300 DPI is for printing. The estimate under the setting shows the image size and roughly how much space the images will take before you start. Images are named after the file and page, for example certificate-page-01.jpg, so they sort in order.

      A portal that wants a photo or signature at an exact pixel size or KB limit needs more than this: convert the page here, then use the image resizer. To keep pages as a PDF instead, use Split PDF, and to copy the words rather than a picture of them, use Extract text from PDF.

      Limits

      Up to 500 pages at a time. Very large pages are drawn at a lower resolution so the image stays within what phones can handle (about 16 million pixels); when that happens, the result says so. The images are pictures: text in them cannot be selected or searched.

      Your files are processed in this browser and never leave your device. Closing the tab clears them.

      Questions

      Which resolution should I choose?

      150 DPI is right for most uses: an A4 page comes out at 1240 × 1754 pixels, sharp on screen and small to send. Choose 300 DPI only for printing, as files are about four times larger. 72 DPI gives the smallest files.

      JPG or PNG?

      JPG for most pages, especially scans and photos: the files are much smaller. PNG keeps the edges of text and lines perfectly crisp, but files are larger. Many portals accept only JPG.

      Can I convert only some pages?

      Yes. Select the pages in the grid, or type them, for example 1, 3-4. With nothing selected, every page is converted.

      How do I get all the images at once?

      Download all as ZIP gives you one ZIP file with every image, named with its page number. You can also download each image on its own.

      Why does the result say the resolution was lowered?

      A very large page at a high resolution would need more pixels than a phone browser can draw. That page is drawn at the highest resolution that fits, and the size is shown with the result.

      Is my PDF uploaded?

      No. Pages are drawn by your browser on this device, and the images are made there too.
