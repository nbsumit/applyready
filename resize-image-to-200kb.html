The Document Upload Standard for Certificates & Marksheets

Whenever Indian recruitment portals and national entrance exams require full-page scans — such as NEET-UG postcard photos (4×6 in), Class 10/12 marksheets, caste/EWS certificates, or domicile proofs — the prescribed maximum size is almost universally 200 KB.

Camera photos taken on modern smartphones routinely measure 5 MB to 15 MB. Uploading them raw is rejected immediately. ApplyReady resizes the pixel canvas to 800 × 1000 pixels and calibrates JPEG compression so that every stamp, signature, and printed grade remains crisp while staying reliably under 200 KB.

Tips for Scanning Marksheets and Certificates

  • Keep Document Straight: Use the rotation controls (90° left/right) if your phone camera saved the certificate in sideways orientation.
  • Crop Out the Background: Crop tightly around the edges of the marksheet paper. Eliminating shadows and floor surfaces preserves more bytes for text clarity.
  • Ensure Clean Contrast: Avoid dim room lighting. Good ambient light ensures the text is pitch black on crisp white paper.

How many pixels can 200 KB afford?

A 200 KB file is 204,800 bytes, or 1,638,400 bits. A scanned page has far more pixels than a passport photo, so this budget is spread thinly, but a page of printed text is easier to compress than a photograph.

Rule of thumb for photographs: 2.5 or more bits per pixel is comfortable, 1.5 to 2.5 is workable, below 1.5 looks soft or blocky.

Text pages follow different rules from photos. The "comfortable" and "tight" labels in the table are a rule of thumb for photographs. A certificate or marksheet is mostly flat white paper with dark text, which compresses far better than skin tones, so a text page can stay readable at fewer bits per pixel. Judge by reading the smallest text on the result, not by the table alone.

Where the bytes go. An A4 page at 150 DPI has about 2.2 million pixels. Staying under 200 KB means each pixel gets under one bit, so keep the page flat and evenly lit, crop away the table around it, and avoid coloured backgrounds, stamps with large solid fills and photographs of screens.

When one page is not enough. A portal that allows 200 KB per file expects one document or one page per upload. If a document has several pages, upload them separately where the form allows, or combine them and check the result against the limit.

  • Photograph or scan flat, straight, in daylight. A clean source saves more bytes than any setting.
  • Read the smallest text on the result: dates, roll numbers and percentages.
  • If a text page is still too soft, try greyscale or a slightly smaller pixel size before lowering the quality further.

Frequently Asked Questions: 200 KB Document Resizing

Common queries on scanned certificate and document limits.

Why do portals cap certificate scans at 200 KB?

200 KB allows adequate resolution for officials to verify serial numbers, roll numbers, and official seals while preventing server storage bottlenecks when millions of applicants upload multiple documents.

Can I use this for NEET 4×6 inch postcard photos?

Yes. NTA NEET specifies that postcard size photographs must be between 10 KB and 200 KB. This tool ensures your postcard photo satisfies the strict 200 KB ceiling.
